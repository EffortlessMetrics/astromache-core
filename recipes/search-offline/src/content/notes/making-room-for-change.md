## Choose a small uncertainty

When a plan feels too large, choose one uncertainty that matters to the next decision. A small experiment can answer a narrow question while leaving the rest of the work alone. State what you expect to learn before choosing the activity.

## Make a comparison possible

Write down the current approach, the proposed change, and a measure that a colleague could recognize. The measure may be time, clarity, or the number of unanswered questions at a handoff. It should help you decide what to do next.

## Agree on a stopping point

A bounded trial has an end. Decide when to review the result and what would make you stop early. A clear stopping point protects both the people doing the work and the usefulness of the evidence.

1. Choose one question.
2. Describe the current approach.
3. Try one change.
4. Compare what happened.
5. Keep, revise, or stop the experiment.

## Keep the explanation

A result is more useful when its limits travel with it. Keep the context beside the observation so someone else can understand what was tested. The next team may need a different answer, but it can still benefit from the question.
